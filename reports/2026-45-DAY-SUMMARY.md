## 2. Security / Exploits

1. **$900M+ stolen in April alone — worst month in crypto history.** Drift Protocol ($285-295M, Solana, DPRK-linked) and Kelp DAO ($292M, LayerZero bridge misconfiguration) were the largest. 30+ incidents in the month. Social engineering overtook smart contract bugs as the primary attack vector.

2. **"DeFi United" cross-protocol bailout created moral hazard precedent.** After Kelp DAO: Ether.fi pledged 5K ETH, Stani 5K ETH, Lido 2.5K stETH, Solana Foundation lent USDT to Aave, Golem 1K ETH. Combined ~$258M rsETH shortfall recovery. First coordinated industry bail-out; opened debate on whether protocols should maintain bail-out expectations.

3. **LayerZero exodus: $4B+ migrated to Chainlink CCIP (mid-May).** Lombard ($1B), Kraken, KelpDAO all left after the Kelp exploit exposed DVN configuration risks. Largest infrastructure migration in DeFi bridge history. Bridge exploits totaled $328M+ in May across 8 incidents (THORChain $10.8M, Verus $11.5M, Cetus $223M on Sui).

4. **Gnosis Pay double-exploit (June 1).** Zodiac delay module attack allowed mass-queuing of malicious transactions. Followed by a separate $3M+ SquidRouterModule exploit the week before. Koppelmann pledged full reimbursement. Demonstrated that security modules can become attack surfaces.

5. **Attack pattern shift: infrastructure > smart contracts.** 63% of May exploit losses came from infrastructure-layer attacks (multisig tampering, bridge verification bypasses, module exploits), not smart contract bugs. NK-linked groups reportedly using AI to select targets. Bitwarden CLI supply chain attack (@bitwarden/cli@2026.4.0) compromised developer CI/CD pipelines.

## 3. Regulation / Policy

1. **CLARITY Act survived a gauntlet — passed Senate Banking Committee 15-9 (May 15).** 100+ amendments filed, bipartisan talks collapsed, banking lobby opposed, 16 anti-DeFi amendments filed by Democrats — all defeated in committee. Key compromise: passive stablecoin yield banned, activity-based rewards ("buy and use" model) permitted. Polymarket odds hit 75%. Senate floor vote still requires 60 votes.

2. **GENIUS Act implementation entered active rulemaking.** FinCEN/OFAC issued joint AML rules for "permitted payment stablecoin issuers" (PPSIs) — treating them as bank-grade financial institutions. Three cascading deadlines: "substantially similar" state regime comments due June 2, AML rules comments due June 9, implementation rules due July 18. These determine who can issue regulated stablecoins in the US.

3. **SEC shifted to notice-and-comment approach.** Chair Atkins signaled evaluating when DeFi protocols qualify as exchanges/broker-dealers, with yield-bearing vaults under particular scrutiny. Crucially, chose rulemaking over enforcement-first. SEC also exempted DeFi frontends from broker-dealer registration.

4. **International moves accelerated.** EU banned Russia/Belarus crypto ecosystem effective May 24 (largest geographic crypto ban by a major economy). Japan reclassified crypto as securities-grade assets. Hong Kong granted first stablecoin licenses to HSBC and Standard Chartered/Animoca JV. DOL proposed opening $10T US 401(k) market to crypto.

5. **US Senate unanimously banned prediction market trading by members (May 1).** Polymarket partnered with Chainalysis for compliance. This paradoxically legitimizes prediction markets as financial instruments. CFTC approved Kalshi's Bitcoin perps (June 1) — first US-regulated BTC perpetual futures.
